# City Curriculum Layout

## Purpose

This document defines the current city-based curriculum layout for the world map.

It is meant to answer one question clearly:

- Is the trip map organized in a way that supports actual beginner learning?

The answer should be:

- yes, if each city groups situations the learner is likely to encounter there
- yes, if the early map emphasizes survival and repetition over novelty
- no, if cities are padded with weak placeholder levels that do not represent real learning value

This document reflects a cleaned-up first pass of the city map.

## Layout Rule

The top-level world map is organized by `city`.

Inside each city are `levels`.

Inside each level are `sublevels`.

The city map should feel like the real trip, but the learning logic should still stay situation-based.

That means:

- cities organize the experience emotionally and visually
- levels organize practical scenario clusters
- sublevels remain the actual playable learning units

## Current Recommended City Layout

### Tokyo

Purpose:

- first arrival city
- highest density of practical beginner interactions
- best place for early survival Japanese and broad reusable systems

Levels:

1. `Arrival`
2. `Restaurant`
3. `City Systems`
4. `Shopping`

Why this works:

- arrival gives the trip a natural opening
- restaurant provides the strongest early emotional payoff
- city systems covers convenience store and trains, which are high-frequency Tokyo needs
- shopping fits Shibuya, Harajuku, Akihabara, and Don Quijote

### Hakone

Purpose:

- special transit and ryokan leg
- etiquette-heavy and itinerary-specific

Levels:

1. `Transit`
2. `Ryokan`
3. `Scenic Day`

Why this works:

- transit reflects Romancecar, loop travel, and route confirmation
- ryokan captures check-in and onsen language
- scenic day handles Lake Ashi, ropeway, and calm sightseeing interactions

### Kyoto

Purpose:

- strongest cultural and etiquette city
- major shrine, market, and traditional dining exposure

Levels:

1. `Temples`
2. `Markets`
3. `Arashiyama`

Why this works:

- temples supports shrine language and etiquette
- markets supports Nishiki browsing and food interactions
- Arashiyama is a clean scenic/timing/tickets branch

### Hiroshima

Purpose:

- guided day trip with transit pressure and unfamiliar-station usage

Levels:

1. `Day Trip`
2. `Evening Return`

Why this works:

- the first half is about outbound travel and daytime logistics
- the second half is about getting back cleanly and handling station return language

This is intentionally small because Hiroshima is a day trip, not a full-city stay.

### Nara

Purpose:

- short sightseeing city with landmarks, deer park, temple visits, and light souvenir/snack interaction

Levels:

1. `Park Day`
2. `Souvenirs`

Why this works:

- park day covers deer, landmarks, walking, and temple context
- souvenirs gives Nara a lighter browsing and snack role without forcing an unrelated return branch

### Osaka

Purpose:

- fast, lively, food-first city with a short visit window

Levels:

1. `Street Food`
2. `City Day`

Why this works:

- street food handles Kuromon and Dotonbori energy well
- city day handles Osaka Castle plus return-to-Tokyo logistics without making the whole city just a transit node

## What Was Cleaned Up

Several placeholder level labels were weak or misleading.

The biggest issues were:

- `Nara` using a `Gion Return` style level, which belongs to Kyoto rather than Nara
- `Hiroshima` and `Osaka` being over-reduced to generic `Return` labels
- a few sublevel names not clearly tied to real itinerary moments

These were adjusted so each city feels more self-contained and aligned with the trip.

## Pedagogical Check

This layout is appropriate if we keep these rules:

1. `Tokyo` remains the main early-learning hub
2. `Restaurant` stays one of the first truly playable gold-standard branches
3. `Hakone`, `Hiroshima`, `Nara`, and `Osaka` stay smaller and more focused
4. cities do not get padded with filler levels just to look balanced

The learner does not need each city to have equal depth.

The learner needs:

- a believable trip structure
- repeated useful patterns
- clear scenario clusters

That is the standard this layout should serve.
