# Arrival Full Curriculum Map

This document defines the first full authored pass for the `Arrival` category in `Tokyo`.

The point of this branch is not to teach every transportation or hotel phrase at once. The point is to make the first hours in Japan survivable:

- understand the most important airport signs
- find the station, restroom, and exit
- handle the first train or taxi transfer into the city
- check into the hotel with a reservation name and passport

## Category Structure

`Tokyo -> Arrival`

Current subcategories:

- `Airport Basics`
- `Transfer to City`
- `Hotel Check-In`

## Airport Basics

Purpose:
- survive the immediate post-landing environment without freezing on signs or simple questions

Internal lesson levels:
- `Airport Signs`
- `Documents and Counters`
- `Finding Key Places`
- `Arrival Review`

What it teaches:
- exit, arrivals, baggage, restroom, station
- passport, customs, immigration, counter, information desk
- where is it, here, over there, taxi, train

## Transfer to City

Purpose:
- get from the airport to Tokyo using train, ticket, or taxi language

Internal lesson levels:
- `Train and Station Basics`
- `Tickets and IC Cards`
- `Taxi Basics`
- `Transfer Review`

What it teaches:
- train, platform, boarding area, ticket, line
- IC card, reload, one way, round trip, ticket machine area
- to this hotel, please; here, please; address; luggage; taxi

## Hotel Check-In

Purpose:
- complete the first hotel interaction without needing full free conversation

Internal lesson levels:
- `Reservation Words`
- `Check-In Phrases`
- `Room and Breakfast`
- `Short Check-In Exchange`

What it teaches:
- reservation, name, room, key, breakfast
- I have a reservation; I am Jared; one night; two people; check in
- check out, elevator, breakfast is at seven, passport please, this way

## Authoring Notes

This branch is intentionally practical and bounded.

It does not try to teach:

- full train troubleshooting
- advanced hotel problems
- all airport paperwork language

Those can become later branches or bonus lessons.

The success condition for this category is simpler:

- the learner can read a few crucial signs
- ask or understand one or two very short help phrases
- survive the first hotel check-in flow
