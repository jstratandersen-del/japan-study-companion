# First-Pass Level Catalog

## Purpose

This document is the first broad catalog of levels across the full world map.

It is intentionally a first pass:

- names are good enough, not final
- ordering is directional, not locked
- some levels may later merge, split, or move

The goal is to answer:

- what locations exist
- what levels likely belong in each location
- which levels feel foundational versus later or optional

This is the map-wide planning layer. It is not yet the detailed sublevel specification.

## Assumptions

- `Sublevels` remain the actual playable units
- this document is only naming `Levels` inside each `Location`
- locations and levels unlock non-linearly across the world map
- some levels are clearly essential, while others are enrichment or trip-specific

## Location Catalog

## 1. Airport

Purpose:

- survive arrival and departure with low panic

Suggested levels:

1. `Arrival Signs and Core Words`
2. `Check-In and Passport Basics`
3. `Queueing, Gates, and Boarding`
4. `Arrival Transit Out of the Airport`
5. `Departure Timing and Terminal Navigation`
6. `Security, Boarding, and Final Questions`

Notes:

- this should be one shared location for both arrival and departure
- levels 1 to 4 matter earlier
- levels 5 and 6 can unlock later closer to return travel

## 2. Hotel and Check-In

Purpose:

- handle hotels smoothly and ask for simple help

Suggested levels:

1. `Reservation and Check-In Basics`
2. `Room, Key, and Hotel Vocabulary`
3. `Breakfast, Timing, and Hotel Services`
4. `Luggage Holding and Delivery`
5. `Asking the Front Desk for Help`
6. `Payment, Checkout, and Final Questions`

Notes:

- front desk help should unlock after basic check-in
- luggage delivery should connect to Hakone and Kyoto travel days

## 3. Convenience Store

Purpose:

- handle the highest-frequency low-stakes purchases confidently

Suggested levels:

1. `Store Words and Food Categories`
2. `Choosing an Item and Paying`
3. `Bag, Heating, and Checkout Questions`
4. `Drinks, Snacks, and Quick Purchases`
5. `Using the Restroom and Asking Where Things Are`

Notes:

- should unlock early
- strongly overlaps with restaurant payment and shopping basics

## 4. Trains and Stations

Purpose:

- make station movement and train selection survivable

Suggested levels:

1. `Station Signs and Core Train Words`
2. `Platform, Exit, and Transfer Basics`
3. `Which Train Do I Take?`
4. `Buying Tickets and Using Passes`
5. `Reserved Seats and Long-Distance Train Basics`
6. `Delays, Wrong Platforms, and Recovery`

Notes:

- one of the highest-priority locations in the entire app
- should connect to Airport, Hakone, Kyoto, Hiroshima, Osaka, and return-to-Tokyo flows

## 5. Directions and Street Navigation

Purpose:

- handle walking directions and neighborhood movement

Suggested levels:

1. `Here, There, and Where?`
2. `Right, Left, Straight, and Near`
3. `Finding a Place on Foot`
4. `Understanding Short Spoken Directions`
5. `Map Help and Route Confirmation`

Notes:

- likely unlocks very early after core question words
- heavily reused in Tokyo, Kyoto, Nara, and Osaka

## 6. Restaurant

Purpose:

- understand menus, order food, and finish the meal cleanly

Suggested levels:

1. `Menu Basics`
2. `Ordering Basics`
3. `Server Interaction`
4. `Paying and Finishing`

Notes:

- already specified in more detail in `restaurant-location-spec.md`

## 7. Cafe and Sweets

Purpose:

- cover lighter, shorter, more casual food interactions

Suggested levels:

1. `Drink and Dessert Words`
2. `Ordering One Drink or Sweet`
3. `Takeout or Here`
4. `Cafe Questions and Simple Changes`
5. `Fast Casual Ordering Flow`

Notes:

- useful for Harajuku crepes, pancakes, cafes, and snack stops
- should reuse much of Restaurant and Convenience Store

## 8. Market and Street Food

Purpose:

- handle food stalls, browsing, and quick ordering

Suggested levels:

1. `Stall Food Words and Categories`
2. `What Is This?`
3. `Ordering One Item Quickly`
4. `Recommendation and Popular Item Questions`
5. `Cash Only, Eat Here, and Street Etiquette`
6. `Short Fast Vendor Interactions`

Notes:

- important for Tsukiji, Nishiki, Kuromon, and similar stops
- should unlock from Restaurant ordering basics

## 9. Shopping and Souvenirs

Purpose:

- ask for items, sizes, colors, and help in stores

Suggested levels:

1. `Store Words, Item Types, and Basic Browsing`
2. `Do You Have This?`
3. `Size, Color, and Different Version Questions`
4. `Trying Something On`
5. `Tax-Free and Payment Questions`
6. `Souvenir Shopping and Recommendation Questions`

Notes:

- important for Don Quijote, Gion, Nishiki, Shinagawa, and general gift shopping

## 10. Shrine and Temple

Purpose:

- understand etiquette and ask limited respectful questions

Suggested levels:

1. `Shrine and Temple Core Words`
2. `Quiet, Shoes, and Restricted Areas`
3. `Photo Permission and Entry Questions`
4. `Omamori, Goshuin, and Souvenir Basics`
5. `Respectful Behavior and Sign Understanding`

Notes:

- strong match for Senso-ji, Meiji Shrine, Fushimi Inari, Kasuga Shrine, Tenryu-ji, and others

## 11. Attraction and Ticketing

Purpose:

- handle tickets, time slots, and entry flow for booked activities

Suggested levels:

1. `Ticket and Entry Words`
2. `Time Slot and Reservation Confirmation`
3. `Where Do I Enter?`
4. `Photo, Rules, and Staff Guidance`
5. `Activity Check-In and Start Instructions`

Notes:

- useful for TeamLab, Skytree, sumo show, workshops, and similar activities

## 12. Ryokan and Onsen

Purpose:

- handle one of the most culturally specific parts of the trip

Suggested levels:

1. `Ryokan Check-In Basics`
2. `Meal Time, Room, and Yukata Vocabulary`
3. `Bath Timing and Onsen Rules`
4. `Private or Shared Bath Questions`
5. `Understanding Onsen Etiquette and Instructions`

Notes:

- special-purpose but high-value because the context is unfamiliar
- should unlock after Hotel and some basic cultural vocabulary

## 13. Kyoto Cultural Etiquette

Purpose:

- handle quieter, more formal, more etiquette-sensitive settings

Suggested levels:

1. `Polite Quiet-Context Language`
2. `Respectful Questions in Cultural Sites`
3. `Formal Dining and Performance Etiquette`
4. `Simple Appreciation and Thanks in Formal Settings`

Notes:

- likely useful for meditation, maiko dinner, kaiseki, temple contexts, and quieter interactions

## 14. Day Trip Navigation

Purpose:

- support more complex movement on long travel days

Suggested levels:

1. `Early Departure and Timing Language`
2. `Multi-Step Route Confirmation`
3. `How Long Does It Take?`
4. `Wrong Train, Delay, and Recovery`
5. `Return Timing and Last-Train Awareness`

Notes:

- supports Hiroshima, Osaka, Nara, Hakone, and airport-return anxiety

## 15. Hakone Transit

Purpose:

- handle the distinctive transport chain in Hakone

Suggested levels:

1. `Romancecar and Reserved Travel Basics`
2. `Cable Car, Ropeway, and Transfer Vocabulary`
3. `Lake Ashi and Scenic Route Questions`
4. `Luggage, Timings, and Route Confirmation`

Notes:

- shorter bonus location
- this could later remain separate or be absorbed into Trains and Day Trip Navigation

## 16. Airport Return and Departure

Purpose:

- reduce stress on the final day

Suggested levels:

1. `Leaving for the Airport on Time`
2. `Confirming the Best Route to Haneda`
3. `Terminal, Check-In, and Bag Drop`
4. `Security, Gate, and Final Boarding`

Notes:

- shorter bonus location
- this could remain folded into Airport later

## 17. Pharmacy and Help

Purpose:

- cover health or confusion moments without overbuilding a full emergency system

Suggested levels:

1. `Body and Symptom Basics`
2. `Asking for Medicine`
3. `Understanding Dosage and Simple Instructions`
4. `Basic Trouble and Help Questions`

Notes:

- probably later-unlock, but important enough to exist

## 18. Group Coordination and Social Navigation

Purpose:

- support moving around with other people and simple meetup coordination

Suggested levels:

1. `Where Are You?`
2. `Let's Meet Here`
3. `I’m Late / I’m Here / Wait a Moment`
4. `Checking the Plan and Timing`

Notes:

- shorter bonus location
- not the highest priority, but likely useful in Shibuya, stations, and group days

## 19. Akihabara and Hobby Shopping

Purpose:

- cover more specific browsing for specialty stores

Suggested levels:

1. `Electronics, Games, and Character Goods Words`
2. `Which Floor / Which Section?`
3. `Do You Have This Character or Item?`
4. `Browsing Without Buying Pressure`

Notes:

- shorter bonus location
- kept separate because specialty shopping has unique vocabulary

## 20. Don Quijote and Big-Store Browsing

Purpose:

- support dense, multi-floor, overwhelming shopping spaces

Suggested levels:

1. `Store Layout and Section Words`
2. `Finding a Product Fast`
3. `Asking Staff Where Something Is`
4. `Tax-Free and Checkout Flow`

Notes:

- shorter bonus location
- highly relevant to your itinerary
- may later merge into Shopping, but worth keeping visible for now
