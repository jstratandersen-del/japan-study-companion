# Curriculum Framework

## Purpose

This document outlines how the curriculum should be organized now that the initial itinerary themes are known.

## Curriculum Structure

The curriculum should be organized into:

- foundation tracks
- area tracks
- itinerary-specific subtracks

## 1. Foundation Tracks

Foundation tracks build reusable language ability.

Suggested foundation tracks:

- Politeness and Greetings
- Core Sentence Patterns
- Questions and Clarification
- Requests and Permission
- Numbers, Prices, and Counting
- Directions and Locations
- Time and Scheduling
- Food and Dining Basics
- Shopping Basics
- Problem Solving and Emergencies

## 2. Area Tracks

Area tracks focus on practical settings the user may encounter.

Suggested starter area tracks:

- Airport
- Train and Transit
- Hotel
- Convenience Store
- Restaurant
- Cafe
- Shopping
- Shrine and Temple
- Street Navigation
- Pharmacy and Help

## 3. Itinerary-Specific Subtracks

The current itinerary already supports custom thematic clusters.

Examples:

- `Shinjuku arrival`: hotel check-in, convenience store, neighborhood orientation
- `Asakusa and Tsukiji`: temple etiquette, market food, souvenir browsing
- `Shibuya and Harajuku`: navigation, sweets ordering, shopping, meeting up
- `Hakone transit`: Romancecar, transfers, ropeway, sightseeing movement
- `Ryokan and onsen`: check-in, timing, etiquette, meal flow
- `Kyoto shrines and markets`: shrine etiquette, Nishiki browsing, quiet-context politeness
- `Nara and Osaka`: attraction flow, street food, quick market ordering

The itinerary should shape:

- vocabulary emphasis
- phrase emphasis
- cultural notes
- priority order

## Level Design Template

Each level should answer:

- What real-world outcome does this lesson support?
- What would the user likely hear?
- What would the user likely need to say?
- What building blocks are required?
- What next situations does this unlock?

Each area should also be designed against four anchor stages:

- `Words`
- `Phrases`
- `Simple Conversation`
- `Natural Conversation`

The curriculum should not assume that one level equals one stage. Most areas will need additional build levels between these stages.

## Example Level Types

- word recognition
- word pairing
- phrase construction
- substitution drills
- listening recognition
- vocabulary in context
- scenario choice
- sentence repair
- guided dialogue
- branching dialogue
- rapid review

## Lesson Writing Guidelines

- prefer natural travel language over textbook formality
- keep phrases adaptable
- teach common variants when they are high-value
- avoid isolated vocabulary lists without scenario context
- connect new phrases to existing grammar/building blocks
- assume no prior Japanese knowledge unless a prerequisite explicitly guarantees it
- do not introduce conversation tasks before enough scaffolded build content exists

## Difficulty Model

Difficulty should come from a combination of:

- number of new concepts
- grammatical complexity
- listening complexity
- context ambiguity

Difficulty should not simply equal later position in the graph.

## Content Prioritization

Prioritize content by a combination of universal travel value and itinerary frequency:

### Tier 1

- greetings
- thanks and apologies
- asking simple questions
- asking for help
- ordering food
- paying
- understanding prices
- finding places
- basic transit

### Tier 2

- shopping details
- hotel interactions
- dietary questions
- asking for recommendations
- checking schedules

### Tier 3

- attraction-specific vocabulary
- culturally specific etiquette language
- niche shopping or hobby vocabulary

## Shared Airport Design

Airport content should be one shared area rather than separate airport places.

Suggested airport sub-stages:

- arrival words and signs
- arrival phrases
- arrival check-in exchange
- departure words and signs
- departure phrases
- departure checkpoint and boarding exchange

## Personalization Strategy

The app should personalize in three ways:

- by itinerary location
- by scenario relevance
- by demonstrated learner weakness

## Itinerary Intake Template

The current itinerary already provides:

- cities
- dates
- neighborhoods
- transportation modes
- hotels
- key attractions
- restaurants or food interests
- shopping interests
- known concerns such as dietary restrictions or navigation anxiety

Additional details that would sharpen content later:

- which optional activities are most likely to happen
- whether luggage forwarding is confirmed
- whether the user expects to rely on trains more than taxis
- dietary preferences or restrictions
- how much kana reading support is desired at the start

## Open Content Decisions

- whether kana-only content should be introduced immediately or gradually
- when to phase out romanization support
- whether cultural context notes should be embedded in levels or separate
- whether to bias early content toward speaking, reading signs, or both
