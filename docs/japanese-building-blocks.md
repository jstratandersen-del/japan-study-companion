# Japanese Building Blocks

## Purpose

This document identifies the beginner-to-early-intermediate Japanese building blocks the app should teach, in a practical order for a complete beginner preparing for travel in Japan.

This is not a full academic grammar syllabus. It is a travel-practical communication syllabus.

## Source Basis

This outline is grounded primarily in:

- Japan Foundation `Irodori` Starter and Elementary 1, which organize beginner Japanese around practical "can-do" communication in Japan
- JNTO travel-language guidance and handbook, which provide tourist-use phrases for directions, transportation, restaurants, accommodation, and shopping

The exact sequencing below is a product decision based on those sources plus the trip itinerary.

## Core Principle

The learner starts with zero Japanese.

So the curriculum must move through this order:

1. recognition of high-frequency words
2. meaning and response words
3. short phrase fragments
4. reusable sentence frames
5. guided exchanges
6. short flexible conversations

The app should not jump directly from vocabulary memorization to open conversation.

## Stage 0: Sound and Reading Support

These are enabling blocks, not standalone "conversation" blocks.

- pronunciation basics
- long vowels and doubled consonants
- hiragana recognition
- katakana recognition
- romaji support at the beginning

Product implication:

- early lessons should allow romaji support
- hiragana should be introduced early
- katakana matters quickly because many travel words are loanwords

## Stage 1: Survival Words

These are the first truly useful items.

### Response and social words

- `hai` - yes
- `iie` - no
- `sumimasen` - excuse me / sorry
- `arigatou gozaimasu` - thank you
- `onegaishimasu` - please
- `daijoubu desu` - it is okay / no thank you / I am fine
- `wakarimasen` - I do not understand
- `mou ichido onegaishimasu` - one more time, please

### Nouns with immediate travel value

- toilet
- station
- train
- bus
- exit
- entrance
- hotel
- ticket
- platform
- menu
- water
- cash
- card

### Very short combinations

- `toire wa?` - toilet?
- `kore` - this
- `sore` - that
- `onegaishimasu` with pointing
- `ikura?` -> then `ikura desu ka?`

## Stage 2: Core Sentence Skeleton

These are the first reusable grammar blocks.

### Identity and topic

- `N desu`
- `watashi wa ... desu`
- `... wa ... desu`

### Question marker

- `... desu ka`

### Basic negative

- `... ja nai desu`
- `... arimasen`

### Demonstratives

- `kore / sore / are`
- `kono / sono / ano + noun`

### Basic location words

- `koko / soko / asoko`

Why this matters:

- this is the minimum structure needed to move from words to short phrases with meaning

## Stage 3: Question and Clarification Blocks

The app should introduce these early because they reduce panic in real travel situations.

### Question words

- `doko` - where
- `nan / nani` - what
- `itsu` - when
- `ikura` - how much
- `dochira` - which way / where (polite)

### Clarification phrases

- `mou ichido onegaishimasu`
- `yukkuri onegaishimasu` - slowly, please
- `eigo no menyuu wa arimasu ka` - do you have an English menu?
- `... wa nihongo de nan to iimasu ka` - how do you say ... in Japanese?

## Stage 4: Polite Requests and Wants

This is the first major bridge into useful travel interaction.

### Request frames

- `... o onegaishimasu`
- `... kudasai`
- `misete kudasai` - please show me
- `oshiete kudasai` - please tell me

### Desire / intention

- `... ga hoshii desu` - I want ...
- `... tai desu` patterns later, such as `ikitai desu`

### Permission / possibility

- `... ii desu ka`
- `... dekimasu ka`

This block supports:

- ordering
- asking for directions
- shopping help
- hotel requests

## Stage 5: Numbers, Prices, Time, and Counting

This is essential for the actual itinerary.

### Numbers

- 1-10
- 100
- 1,000

### Price questions

- `ikura desu ka`

### Time

- clock times
- `ji`
- `fun / pun`
- `nanji desu ka`

### Travel quantities

- `hitori / futari`
- counters for nights, tickets, floors, items only as needed

Product implication:

- counting should be taught functionally inside shopping, trains, hotel nights, and food ordering, not as isolated memorization only

## Stage 6: Existence, Location, and Movement

This is another major travel block.

### Existence

- `arimasu`
- `imasu`

### Location pattern

- `... wa doko desu ka`
- `... wa koko ni arimasu`

### Direction words

- right
- left
- straight
- near
- inside
- outside
- upstairs / downstairs

### Movement verbs

- `ikimasu` - go
- `kimasu` - come
- `kaerimasu` - return

This block is central for:

- stations
- airports
- hotels
- attractions

## Stage 7: Practical Verb Patterns

This is where the learner starts to combine more than one meaning.

### Common travel verbs

- go
- come
- eat
- drink
- buy
- see
- wait
- pay
- use
- board / ride
- get off

### Useful verb patterns

- present polite
- past polite
- negative polite
- `V-te kudasai`
- `V-te mo ii desu ka`

Product implication:

- verb teaching should be narrow and contextual
- teach only the verbs needed for the next scenarios

## Stage 8: Adjectives and Descriptions

Needed for real comprehension, not just speaking.

- big / small
- hot / cold
- expensive / cheap
- near / far
- busy / crowded
- delicious
- quiet

These support:

- room issues
- shopping sizes
- food reactions
- directions

## Stage 9: Joining and Sequencing

Needed before conversation feels natural.

- `... to ...` for listing
- `... de ...` in simple travel contexts
- `... kara ...` for because / from
- `... then ...` style sequencing
- simple command chains such as go straight and turn left

This block is especially important for:

- directions
- route explanations
- step-by-step tasks

## Stage 10: Guided Conversation Blocks

Once enough vocabulary and frames exist, the app can move into short exchanges.

### Exchange types

- greeting + request
- question + answer
- purchase + quantity + payment
- destination + route + confirmation
- check-in + reservation + passport

### Conversation requirements

The learner should already know:

- question words
- request frames
- basic verbs
- numbers or time if relevant
- at least one clarification phrase

## Stage 11: Natural Conversation Blocks

This is still bounded travel Japanese, not general fluency.

Needed additions:

- alternate phrasings
- faster service language recognition
- polite responses from staff
- follow-up questions
- basic repair strategies when the first answer is not understood

This stage focuses more on understanding than speaking complexity.

## Recommended Foundation Track Order

The app should likely teach building blocks in this broad order:

1. greetings and social words
2. this / that / where / what
3. `desu` and `desu ka`
4. apology, thanks, and clarification
5. requests with `onegaishimasu` and `kudasai`
6. numbers and prices
7. time and schedules
8. location and directions
9. travel verbs
10. simple negatives and problem statements
11. description adjectives
12. multi-step instructions and conversation repair

## What Should Not Be Front-Loaded

These can wait until they are needed:

- broad kanji study
- complex conjugation charts
- advanced casual speech
- abstract grammar terminology
- large counter systems beyond immediate use cases

## Curriculum Implication

The app should treat Japanese building blocks in two categories:

- global foundations that unlock many areas
- area-specific language that is best taught in context

That mapping is captured in `curriculum-synthesis.md`.
